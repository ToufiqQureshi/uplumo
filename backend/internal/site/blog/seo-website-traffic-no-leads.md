**Short answer:** If your website gets visits but few enquiries, check the path from page to action before buying more traffic. Make the service clear, show proof, answer common objections, and make it easy to call or send a short enquiry from a phone.

![A visitor journey from a clear service page through trust proof to a simple enquiry action](/site/blog/traffic-to-leads.svg)

## First check whether this is a traffic or a conversion problem

Open Google Search Console and check clicks to the pages that should bring customers: service pages, location pages, pricing pages and contact pages. Then check your analytics for visits and enquiry events. Search Console shows what happened in Google Search; analytics shows what visitors did after they arrived.

If clicks are low, improve discoverability and the search result. If clicks are healthy but people leave without contacting you, inspect the page and enquiry journey. Do not treat every problem as “we need more SEO”.

## Five gaps that stop a visit becoming an enquiry

### 1. The page does not say what you do

A visitor should understand the service, who it is for and where you provide it within a few seconds. Replace vague lines such as “We create better experiences” with a clear sentence: “We repair split air conditioners for homes in Pune.”

### 2. There is no next step

Put one clear action near the top and repeat it after the details. Use a phone link on mobile, a short enquiry form, or a booking button. Do not ask for ten fields before you have earned the visitor’s trust.

### 3. The page has no proof

Show real examples, qualifications, service areas, project photos or customer feedback that you have permission to publish. Explain what the example shows. A row of logos without context is weaker than a short case example with the problem, work done and result.

### 4. Important questions stay unanswered

Add practical information about price ranges, timing, availability, what is included and what happens after someone enquires. If prices vary, explain why and offer a simple way to get an estimate.

### 5. The mobile experience creates friction

Test the page on a phone with one hand. Check that the call button works, the form is readable, the page loads, and pop-ups do not cover the content. Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to try to contact you and watch where they hesitate.

## Use this one-page repair checklist

For each important service page, record:

- The search that should lead someone here.
- The service and service area in one plain sentence.
- One primary action, such as call, book or request a quote.
- Two or three pieces of genuine proof.
- The top questions a buyer asks before choosing.
- Mobile loading, form completion and working phone links.
- Enquiries from this page, not only total visits.

Change one or two major things at a time. Compare enquiry rates over a useful period and remember that small counts can swing sharply from week to week.

## A simple example

A local dentist sees 600 monthly visits to its teeth-whitening page and gets four enquiries. The page has a generic headline, no treatment explanation and a form asking for the visitor’s full address. The clinic can first clarify who the treatment suits, explain how an appointment works, show a real clinic photo and shorten the form to name, phone and preferred time. Then it can compare calls and form submissions with the previous period.

This does not guarantee a particular increase. It makes the page easier to understand and the result easier to measure.

## When to ask for professional help

Get a second pair of eyes if tracking is missing, pages are slow or broken, visitors cannot complete forms, or you have changed the site without knowing what happened. Bring the page URL, the goal and a few weeks of traffic and enquiry data. That makes advice more useful than a general request to “do SEO”.

## Check lead quality, not just form count

A page can produce many enquiries and still fail the business if they are outside the service area or asking for work you do not offer. Add one simple field that helps qualify the request, such as service type or postcode, but do not make the form unnecessarily long. Review a few real enquiries with the sales or service team. They can tell you whether the page attracts the right people.

If calls matter, use a tracked phone link where privacy rules and your setup allow it. Keep a backup record of calls and forms so a tracking glitch does not make the business look quieter than it is. Separate spam from genuine enquiries and note how each lead was answered. That shows whether the problem is the page, the audience or the follow-up process.

A practical monthly comparison is: relevant page visits, completed calls or forms, qualified leads, and booked work. The path from search to sale can take more than one visit, so avoid claiming that one page caused a sale unless you can trace it.

## Find the leaking step in the enquiry funnel

Track the stages separately:

```text
Relevant landing-page visit -> Contact action -> Successful submission
-> Genuine enquiry -> Qualified lead -> Booked work
```

The following numbers are hypothetical: 500 relevant sessions, 40 form starts, 10 successful submissions, 6 qualified leads and 2 bookings. The session-to-submission rate is 2%; the session-to-qualified-lead rate is 1.2%. Calling all 40 form starts “leads” would hide a substantial completion gap.

| Largest drop | Check first |
| --- | --- |
| Visits to contact actions | Intent, offer, service area, proof and next-step clarity |
| Form starts to successful submissions | Validation, required fields, mobile errors and server response |
| Submissions to genuine enquiries | Spam, duplicate events and actual delivery to the business |
| Genuine to qualified | Audience fit, coverage, affordability and service eligibility |
| Qualified to booked | Response time, availability, estimate process and follow-up |

Test a form end to end with clearly marked test data: visible confirmation, server acceptance and delivery to the correct person. Count a success after the system confirms it, not merely when the submit button is clicked. A phone-link click and a WhatsApp click are intent signals, not proof of a connected conversation or sale.

Review source and landing page alongside qualification. A broad tutorial may attract DIY readers who never wanted to hire you; shortening the form will not change that intent. Link to the professional offer where relevant and judge the guide by its actual role.

With small counts, compare longer periods and read the enquiries. Avoid declaring an optimization winner from two submissions or importing a generic conversion-rate target from an unrelated industry.

## FAQ

**Should I buy more traffic first?**

Only after you know the current traffic reaches the right page and can complete the action. More visits to a confusing page can mean more of the same problem.

**How many calls should a site get?**

There is no useful universal number. It depends on the service, visitor intent, location, season and how you count a qualified enquiry.

**Can an SEO tool fix conversion problems?**

A tool can find issues and help you track pages. Your offer, proof and enquiry process still need to make sense to a real customer.
