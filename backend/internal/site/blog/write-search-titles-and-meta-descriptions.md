**Short answer:** Write a unique title that accurately names the page, then write a clear description that helps a searcher decide whether to visit. Use the service, audience or benefit where it fits naturally. Google may rewrite the title link or snippet, so make sure the visible page supports what you promise.

![A search result with a clear title, useful description and page that delivers on the promise](/site/blog/search-result-title-description.svg)

## What a title and description do

The page title helps people and search engines understand what a page is about. Google can use it as one source for the title link shown in results, but it may choose other page text if that better represents the page. A meta description can be used for the snippet, but Google may create a different snippet for a specific search.

These elements can improve clarity and the chance that a relevant person clicks. They do not guarantee rankings or a particular snippet.

## A title-writing pattern

Use this simple order:

**Main page topic + useful detail + business name, if it fits**

Examples:

- “Emergency plumber in Surat | Riverbend Plumbing”
- “Pune wedding photography packages | Lumen Studio”
- “How to choose a laptop for school | Bright Tech”

Keep the wording natural. Do not repeat the same phrase several times or promise something the page does not offer.

## A description-writing pattern

Try one or two short sentences:

1. Say who the page is for or what it helps with.
2. Add a real detail that makes the page useful.
3. Give the next step if there is one.

For example: “Need a plumber in Surat today? See our repair services, areas covered and what to expect when you request a visit.”

Do not put a list of keywords in the description. Do not claim “lowest prices” or “guaranteed same-day service” unless it is true and available to every relevant visitor.

## Fix low-click pages with evidence

In Search Console, look for pages that receive impressions for relevant queries but fewer clicks than you expect. Read the actual queries and ask:

- Does the title explain the correct service?
- Is the description specific to this page?
- Does the result match what the page provides?
- Is the page’s main heading consistent with the title?
- Are competing pages on your own site sending a confusing message?

Change one page, note the date, and review later. A search result can change for reasons outside your control, including the query, device and result layout.

## Keep titles and descriptions unique

A common problem on small sites is that every page says “Home | Company Name” or repeats the same generic description. Give important pages distinct titles based on what they do. You do not need to rewrite every page at once; start with the pages that bring leads or are gaining impressions.

## Keep a record when you change a snippet

Save the URL, queries, old title and description, new wording, country and date. If the page serves different markets, avoid implying that one English description is the best wording for every audience. Use accurate language and currencies on local pages.

Wait for the result to be recrawled and for enough impressions to accumulate before judging the change. A better click-through rate can be useful, but check whether the clicks are relevant and whether the page creates enquiries. Avoid clickbait that wins a visit and disappoints the customer.

If Google keeps choosing a different title, inspect the headings, prominent text and internal anchor text. Make sure they describe the page consistently instead of trying to force one exact phrase.

## Write different promises for different page types

These examples are fictional and should be adapted to real offers:

| Page | Weak title | Clearer title |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service | “Services — Acme” | “Split AC repair in Pune — Acme” |
| Product | “Best bottles online cheap” | “750 ml insulated steel bottle — Acme” |
| Troubleshooting guide | “Everything about Google SEO” | “Website not showing on Google? Check indexing first” |
| Comparison guide | “Best software guaranteed” | “How to compare AI visibility tools: data, limits and cost” |

A matching service-page description could say: “See the repairs we handle, areas covered and how to request an inspection. Share your AC model and symptoms for the next step.” Do not add a guaranteed arrival time or price if the business cannot deliver it.

Implement the values in the proper HTML fields, for example:

```html
<title>Split AC repair in Pune — Acme</title>
<meta name="description"
      content="See repairs covered, service areas and how to request an AC inspection.">
```

Search result display is limited by layout and pixel width, not a universal character quota. Your CMS may impose its own editorial bounds, but those are not Google's guaranteed display limits. Meta descriptions are not a direct ranking lever; use them to summarize the page accurately for relevant searchers.

If Google rewrites the title, check whether the visible H1, prominent text and incoming anchors send a different message. If the snippet differs by query, Google may have selected a passage that better answers that query. Record the query, device, date and market before treating a rewrite as a defect.

Evaluate wording changes with the same query group and comparable positions. A CTR rise caused by improved ranking is not evidence that the description alone worked.

## FAQ

**Is there a fixed character limit for titles?**

Google can display different title lengths across layouts and devices. Aim for concise, descriptive wording instead of filling a character quota.

**Can I force Google to show my meta description?**

No. Google may use your description or other page text depending on the search.

**Should I put my city in every title?**

Use the location when it accurately describes the page and helps a customer. Do not add cities where you do not serve.
