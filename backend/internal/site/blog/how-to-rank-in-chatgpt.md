**To rank in ChatGPT, let `OAI-SearchBot` fetch your public pages, state clearly who you serve and what you offer, make sure trusted third-party sources describe you the same way, and publish pages that answer the exact comparisons buyers ask.** ChatGPT has no fixed ranking like Google's ten blue links. When it searches the web, it builds an answer from pages it can fetch and trust, and it names the businesses those pages support.

So “ranking” in ChatGPT means two things you can measure: how often your business is **mentioned or recommended** for a set of buyer questions, and how often your site is **cited** as a source.

## How ChatGPT decides who to recommend

![Four things behind a ChatGPT recommendation: access, clarity, corroboration and fit](/site/blog/rank-in-chatgpt-signals.webp)

*A recommendation needs all four. Missing one is usually why a competitor appears instead.*

| Signal | What it means | Quick check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Access** | ChatGPT search can fetch your page | `robots.txt` and your CDN allow `OAI-SearchBot`; the page returns HTML text |
| **Clarity** | Your page states who you serve, where, what you offer and at what price range | Could a stranger summarize your offer from the first screen? |
| **Corroboration** | Directories, reviews, articles and comparison sites describe you consistently | Search your brand plus your category and read the top results |
| **Fit** | A page matches the buyer's exact question | Do you have a page for “X for Y” or “X vs Y” questions buyers ask? |

OpenAI documents `OAI-SearchBot` for ChatGPT search and `GPTBot` for possible training. They are controlled separately. Blocking `GPTBot` does not block search. [OpenAI crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)

## Seven steps to rank in ChatGPT

### 1. Allow ChatGPT search to read your site

Check that `robots.txt` does not block `OAI-SearchBot`, and that your CDN or firewall does not serve it a challenge page. Fetch your key pages without being logged in and confirm the main text is in the HTML. The [AI citation checklist](/blog/how-to-get-website-cited-by-ai) covers each check.

### 2. Say exactly what you are, on your own site

Your homepage and key service pages should state, in plain text: what you sell, who it is for, where you operate, typical price range or how pricing works, and what makes you different. Vague copy such as “innovative solutions for modern businesses” gives ChatGPT nothing to repeat.

### 3. Build pages for the questions buyers ask

Buyers ask ChatGPT for shortlists and comparisons: “best project tool for small agencies”, “X vs Y for remote teams”, “affordable family hotel near the beach with a kids' club”. Publish honest comparison, use-case and pricing pages that answer those questions with specifics. Put the answer first, as explained in [what AEO is](/blog/what-is-aeo).

### 4. Get described consistently elsewhere

ChatGPT often cites directories, review platforms, industry lists and news articles. Claim and complete your listings, keep name, category, location and offer consistent, and request corrections where a source is wrong. Earn mentions in relevant roundups by being genuinely useful to the people who write them.

### 5. Collect reviews that mention specifics

Reviews that say what the customer bought and why it worked give AI answers concrete details to repeat. Ask customers to mention the service, the problem it solved and the setting.

### 6. Keep facts fresh

Out-of-date prices, hours or policies on any page can be repeated in an answer. Update the official page first, then the listings you control, and date important facts.

### 7. Track a fixed set of questions

Pick 10 to 20 buyer questions and check them repeatedly. Record whether you are mentioned, recommended or cited, and which competitors and sources appear. If a competitor keeps winning, work through [why ChatGPT recommends competitors](/blog/chatgpt-recommends-competitors-not-my-business).

## How to check your ChatGPT ranking

1. **Write the questions.** Use unbranded buyer questions, not “What is [brand]?”.
2. **Run them repeatedly.** Answers vary between runs. Use fresh chats, or a tool that samples the same prompts on a schedule.
3. **Record three things per answer.** Were you mentioned? Were you recommended? Was your site linked?
4. **Calculate rates.** Mentions divided by valid answers gives a mention rate you can compare week to week. Our guide to [tracking competitors in AI search](/blog/how-to-track-competitors-in-ai-search) shows the full method.
5. **Connect it to visits.** Check ChatGPT referrals and the leads they produce in Uplumo Analytics or [GA4](/blog/chatgpt-traffic-google-analytics).

## What does not work

- **Keyword stuffing or hidden text.** ChatGPT search relies on pages that read well to people.
- **Buying fake reviews or paid mentions.** They risk platform penalties and can be called out in answers.
- **Special AI files as a shortcut.** There is no file that makes ChatGPT recommend you. Access and clear, corroborated facts do the work.
- **Judging by one screenshot.** One run is not a ranking. Track a panel over time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pay to rank in ChatGPT?

There is no published way to buy a place inside ChatGPT's answers. Improve your pages and listings, then measure the results.

### How long does it take to show up in ChatGPT?

ChatGPT search needs to fetch the updated page, and third-party sources need to reflect changes. Expect to track for several weeks before you see a stable change in mention or citation rate.

### Does ranking on Google help with ChatGPT?

Often, because the same clear, crawlable and well-linked pages tend to be retrieved by both. ChatGPT also uses other sources, so a top Google ranking does not guarantee a ChatGPT recommendation.

### Is ranking in ChatGPT the same as GEO?

Yes, it is one part of GEO (generative engine optimization), which covers how all AI assistants describe and cite you. See [SEO vs AEO vs GEO](/blog/seo-vs-aeo-vs-geo).
