# How the Google Search page works

1. **Connect Search Console.** Until a property is connected, the page only
   shows the connection card.
2. **Pick the dates.** The date list offers Last 7 days, Last 28 days (the
   default), Last 3, 6, 12 or 16 months, This month and Last month. For any
   other range, choose From and To and press Apply (for example 1 to 31
   October). Google keeps 16 months and its newest data is about three days
   old, so the dates stay inside that.
3. **Read the four numbers.** Clicks, impressions, CTR and average position,
   each with its change against the period just before. A lower position is
   better, so a drop in position shows as good.
4. **Read "What changed".** Up to four lines from your own data: the keyword
   that gained the most clicks, the keyword and the page that lost the most
   (and the plain reason: position fell, fewer searches, or fewer clicks on
   your result), which country brings most of your clicks, and how many
   searches sit on positions 5 to 20. A line only appears when the data
   supports it; with nothing to say, the card is hidden. Choosing a line
   opens the rows behind it.
5. **Use the tables.**
   - **Keywords** and **Pages**: every search or page with clicks,
     impressions, CTR and position, page by page, with export to CSV or
     Google Sheets.
   - **Countries** and **Devices**: where and on what your visitors search,
     with each one's share of your clicks. Choose a country or device to see
     the whole page for it.
6. **IndexNow.** The IndexNow tab asks Bing and other IndexNow engines to
   re-crawl your pages after a change. It works without Search Console, so
   it also shows before Google is connected.
7. **Narrow down.** Filters for search type, device, country, and page or
   keyword text (contains, equals, or a regular expression) apply to every
   number and table on the page.

Everything on this page is a free read of your Search Console data; it
costs no credits.
